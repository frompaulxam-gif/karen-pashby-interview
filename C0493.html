INTERVIEWER: So my background is in primary teaching and primary leadership, and I'm really interested in thinking about this from a primary school perspective as well. Are there ways then to make it so that it's not tokenistic in our primary schools?
KAREN: Yeah, I mean, just starting from that idea that it might be tokenistic is a good starting point. I think what's very difficult about this is we're never going to get it right. That's not the point. So what we're really talking about here is reflexive practice, which is a really important quality of all education. And this type of reflexive practice kind of takes it to that next scale, that global scale. Like, how are what we're doing in schools connected to, and hopefully making, the wider world a better place? And so that does not have an age limit, right? And we've really seen a lot of examples of how primary school teachers are doing quite amazing work in this area. So I don't think age lets us off the hook. But of course, good teachers understand how to have good education that's age appropriate.
INTERVIEWER: So it sounds to me like it's quite connected to values. And so what values come into this kind of work?
KAREN: Well, one of the values I think is multiple perspectivity. But it's not just about lining up a bunch of different perspectives. It's as much about recognizing whose perspectives you don't know, or perhaps can't know. And becoming much more comfortable with that humility and that understanding that you don't know everything. Because I think we're increasingly realizing that that is a key element of today's world — is that uncertainty. But in education, that hasn't been something we've been that great at taking up. We want to help our students come up with the answers and come up with the actions. Well, they will come up with answers and actions. They want to do things. They want to make the world a better place. But it's our job, I think, as educators, to make sure that they're also learning to critically reflect, think through what they do know and they don't know about a particular issue, and account for that in their response. And then reflect critically on whatever action they took or whatever response they want to put forward.
INTERVIEWER: Many thanks, Karen.
